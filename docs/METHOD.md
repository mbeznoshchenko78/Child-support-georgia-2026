# Calculation method · Georgia, November 24, 2026

## Authority and version

| Worksheet roles · (a)(9), (14), (17.2) | The parent with less residence is noncustodial. Equal residence makes the higher **gross** earner noncustodial. Equal gross requires a court designation. A separate residence input is available when residence differs from ordered days. Payer follows the signed calculated transfer. |
| Schedule A · (f)(1)–(4) | Enter annualized includable income, including wages, bonus, commissions, recognized equity, investment and other sources. Each entered source converts to monthly cents. Variable income should be averaged over a reasonable evidence-based time. No automatic vesting, imputation or attribution to another adult. |
| Business income · (f)(1)(B) | Annual gross receipts less allowable expenses plus nonallowable amounts already deducted. Ordinary costs producing income may qualify. Excessive/personal expenses and certain depreciation require review. A negative business result is disclosed and contributes zero to Schedule A, following the Commission business-entry convention. |
| Schedule B · (f)(5)(A) | One-half of self-employment taxes on separately entered taxable SE earnings. Compute 6.2% up to the 2026 OASDI ceiling of $184,500 annually ($15,375 monthly, $953.25 maximum monthly deduction component), plus 1.45% Medicare without that ceiling. Round each component to cents. Taxable SE earnings and guideline business profit are separate inputs. |
| Earlier orders · (a)(18), (f)(5)(B) | Average actually paid current support for other children under orders with earlier initial filing priority. Use 12 months or shorter order life. Arrears payments are excluded. With different order periods, compute the individual averages and enter their sum using a one-month divisor. |
| Qualified children · (a)(20), (f)(5)(C)–(D) | If the court permits, find the theoretical table obligation on that parent’s own gross less the SE adjustment only, times 75%. Apply the allowed qualified-child and preexisting adjustments to gross. No negative adjusted-income calculation is issued. |
| BCSO · (b)(4), (i)(2)(A), (o) | Look up all 785 $50 rows for 1–6 children. Use nearest, with higher row at an exact midpoint per the public calculator. Select $800 below the table and $40,000 above it. Actual adjusted income is retained throughout. No interpolation. |
| Schedule C · (a)(17.1), (g) | Average ordered days over two years. Use overnights or all hours divided by 24 for appropriate recurring daytime periods. Omit the adjustment without an order. Use average days across children with the same custodial situation. Split custody uses separate worksheets. Official inputs total 365, with up to 182.5 noncustodial days. |
| Schedule D · (h)(1)–(2) | Add the prorated child-only medical premium and eligible work-, training- or education-related care, then credit documented costs paid by that parent. A family premium of unknown child-only cost is allocated across covered people. Exclude employer-paid premiums and childcare subsidies. Variable care may be separately ordered. |
| Schedule E · (b)(7), (i) | Enter specific signed proposed adjustments to the noncustodial column. Dental/vision can be modeled as a discretionary pro rata allocation. Extraordinary education net of aid and qualifying extreme medical hardship are credited to payers. Only the portion of special child-rearing expenses above 7% of BCSO is modeled. A court must consider evidence, necessity and written findings. |
| Future uninsured care · (a)(11.1), (h)(3) | Ordinary future uninsured therapy, orthodontia and other care are separately budgeted and allocated pro rata unless ordered otherwise. They do not enter line 10. Avoid counting the same cost in a hardship proposal. |
| Low income · (b)(7)–(9), (i.1), (p) | Compare each relevant positive obligation with its individual income/child-count ceiling. Up through $1,500 apply the published percentage. Above $1,500 use the printed dollar rows. The statutory-first model applies deviations before this ceiling and excludes the terminal income in each column. See differences below. |
| Final payment · (a)(11), (b)(9)–(10) | A positive noncustodial result makes that parent the payer. A negative amount makes the custodial parent pay the magnitude. An eligible NCP Title II or VA derivative credit reduces a positive obligation to a minimum of zero. Credits cannot create a reversal. There is no universal minimum transfer in this model. |

## Parenting-time arithmetic and rounding

Let N and C denote the noncustodial and custodial dollar shares of BCSO. Let n and c be their ordered days. The amount deducted from N equals:

```text
N + (n^2.5 × C − c^2.5 × N) / (n^2.5 + c^2.5)
```

The expression is also equal to the sum of the two dollar shares times n^2.5 / (n^2.5 + c^2.5). Keep the unrounded credit for the Schedule D subtotal. Show the credit to cents. Days may be fractional. For example the Commission shows $669.57 noncustodial and $1,107.43 custodial shares, 120 and 245 days respectively, a $255.46 adjustment and $414.11 presumptive. [Commission’s examples](https://csc.georgiacourts.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Using-Parenting-Time-Adj-and-Low-Income-Adj.pdf)

Pro rata percentages are rounded independently to two decimals as percentages before multiplying by BCSO. The public client uses separate operation orders for the worksheet’s basic share and the Schedule C share at a binary half-cent. The engine preserves those orders and the worksheet’s fixed-two-decimal conversion for Schedule D allocations. Display amounts are cents. The final payment summary rounds the absolute magnitude to a whole dollar, then assigns the payer. An exact negative half-dollar can give a different result if the signed amount is rounded before taking its absolute value. The official printed numeric column and its payer summary can differ on that edge. The app uses the payer-summary convention. [Public calculator release used in validation](https://csconlinecalc.georgiacourts.gov/static/js/main.56d6114c.js)

## Differences that can change a case

The statute orders deviations before low-income adjustment and conditions eligibility on being *less than* the terminal income for the relevant child column. The public worksheet client applies low income on line 11 before proposed deviations on line 12 and includes the exact terminal income. The screen and report show the second signed result when it changes the amount. Either comparison is provisional for a hearing. Use the official filing worksheet and resolve the different treatments with counsel.

For the public one-child $800/$1,261.50 low-income example, model an additional $50 upward deviation. The statute-first amount remains $239.69. The public display code yields $289.69 before whole-dollar rounding. A different case, with a $2,450 NCP at the threshold and $800 of care paid by the other parent, yields $1,022.54 versus $467. A negative noncustodial figure is preserved through the cap step and reversed when assigning the transfer. The app does not cap the magnitude of a reversed transfer by the custodial parent’s separate worksheet-column ceiling. ## Discretion, history and scope

High-income parents retain the top statutory BCSO. A court may consider an upward deviation with the necessary findings. A modeled marginal percentage applies to excess monthly combined adjusted income in a separately labeled planning outcome. An independent negotiated figure reflects only the user’s assumption. Neither is a judicial forecast. Employer equity, timing and averaging are evidence-dependent. Georgia appellate authority has rejected categorically omitting stock grants. [Byers v. Byers, June 8, 2026](https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/court-of-appeals/2026/a26a0381.html)

Ordinary accrued installments cannot generally be rewritten retrospectively. An earlier physical custody change or filing date does not itself establish an increased arrearage. Section (j) addresses a narrow qualifying involuntary income loss of at least 25% and service, and (k)(4) permits temporary relief on motion. Initial paternity past-support claims raise distinct historical-evidence and guidelines issues. The dated model uses calendar-day proration only as a user assumption. It does not accrue statutory interest, determine enforceability or pick a lawful effective date. [Perez v. Cunningham](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ga-court-of-appeals/2069030.html) · [Wheeler v. Akins](https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/court-of-appeals/2014/a14a0075.html) · [Day v. Mason, revised opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ga-court-of-appeals/2096350.html)
